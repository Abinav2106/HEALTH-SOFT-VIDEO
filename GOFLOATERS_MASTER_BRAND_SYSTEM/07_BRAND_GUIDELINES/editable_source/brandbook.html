Part I · The brand02 · Brand idea
Work is no longer
a place you go.
It’s a place you choose.

GoFloaters helps people find flexible spaces to work from. The brand’s job is to make that freedom feel light, friendly and dependable — a dog who knows where the good desks are, and an aircraft that gets you there.

Tagline (unchanged): Spaces that work for you.
Creative territory Proposed Freedom in Motion — an organising idea for campaigns and motion, never a replacement for the tagline.

Part I · The brand03 · Existing identity
The identity we start from

The supplied logo is the source of truth. Everything in this book is built from a vector reconstruction of it.

1 · The dog. Slim, white, hand-inked; long muzzle, small black nose, swept-back ears, orange eye ring.
2 · The aircraft. Turquoise single-prop plane with a yellow propeller disc and a cockpit seat.
3 · Wordmark + speed lines. “Go” bold, “Floaters” regular, grey tagline; five rounded speed lines.

Part III · The character10 · The mascot
The original mascot

Verified from the supplied logo — preserve all of these.

  • Slim white body, hand-inked dark outline (~0.9% of symbol width)
  • Elongated muzzle, small rounded black nose
  • Two swept-back ears, near ear lower
  • Orange ring around the eye (the only accessory)
  • Long upright neck, seated posture
  • Forelegs reaching forward, paws resting
  • Curved tail rising behind
  • Small three-stroke fur marks on the haunch

Never: puppy proportions, fluffy fur, big goggles, clothing, scarves or shoes.

Part III · The character11 · Anatomy and construction
Construction

Layered parts

Head (traced) · two ears (traced, hidden white roots) · procedural neck · torso (traced) · near and far forelegs, tail (redrawn as centre-line “tubes” matched to the source) · eye (white, pupil, two lids) · mouth (traced smile ↔ procedural shapes).

Pivots

Orange dots mark every joint: head, ears, hip/lean, shoulders, wrist, tail root, propeller hub, wing root and the seat anchor that keeps the dog in the cockpit.

Line weight

7.6 px at master scale (2000 px wide lock-up). Redrawn limbs use the same weight so joints never thicken.

Part IV · Motion17 · Motion principles
Motion principles
01

Eyes lead

Pupils move first; the head follows 3–5 frames later.

02

Ears follow

Ears trail the head by ~4 frames, small amplitude (±5°).

03

Never all at once

Aircraft, dog, propeller and trails start and settle on different frames.

04

Glide, then settle

Ease-out arrivals; a soft overshoot on the aircraft only.

05

Land on the logo

Every brand sequence resolves to the master lock-up.

Part V · Visual language28 · Composition
Composition principles
Big type,
left.

Headline left, flight right

The aircraft always travels towards the open side of the layout.

Call to action

The mascot directs attention

Eyes and paw point at the message; never at the edge of the page.

Air to breathe

Keep at least 40% negative space. Patterns sit back; the logo sits forward.

Production record39 · Source notes
Source notes and production record

What was inspected

The supplied 2000×667 GoFloaters logo (PNG/WebP). The Navi by VNGGames Behance project could not be opened from this production environment (network policy blocked behance.net), and no PDF or screenshots of it were supplied. No description of its panels is given here, and nothing in this book copies it.

Original vs proposed

Original: the logo, its colours, the dog, the aircraft, the wordmark, tagline and speed lines (reconstructed in vector). Proposed: everything tagged Proposed — the creative territory, supporting colours, typography, patterns, logo variants, constructed body views, the game and screen concepts.

Fidelity

Reconstruction vs source at identical size: mean difference 1.971/255; 98.7% of outline pixels within 1 px. The rig’s rest pose matches the master to 0.447/255; the limbs and tail are redrawn as centre-line tubes so they can bend.
